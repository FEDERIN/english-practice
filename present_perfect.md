# Present Perfect

## When to use it

1. **Actions completed at an unspecified time in the past** (result matters now).
   - I have lost my keys. (I don't have them now.)
   - She has finished the report.

2. **Experiences in life** (without saying when).
   - I have been to Japan.
   - Have you ever eaten sushi?

3. **Actions that started in the past and continue to the present**.
   - I have lived here for 5 years.
   - She has worked at this company since 2020.

4. **Recent actions with "just", "already", "yet"**.
   - I have just finished.
   - Have you eaten yet?

## Form

- **Affirmative**: have/has + past participle.
  - I/you/we/they + have + V3 (worked, gone, seen).
  - He/she/it + has + V3.

- **Negative**: haven't/hasn't + past participle.
  - I haven't finished.
  - She hasn't called.

- **Question**: Have/Has + subject + past participle?
  - Have you finished?
  - Has he arrived?

## Common time markers

ever, never, already, yet, just, for, since, recently, lately, so far, up to now.

## Examples

- We have finished the homework.
- We've finished the homework.
- We have lived in Colombia.
- You have learned many words.
- We have visited Mexico.
- We have bought the tickets.
- We have been here for 10 years.
- I have visited that restaurant every Monday.
- I have visited that restaurant several times.
- My mom has prepared lunch.

## Watch out

- Do not use the present perfect with specific past time markers (yesterday, last week, in 2020). Use the simple past instead.
  - I went to Paris last year. (not "I have gone to Paris last year.")
- "For" is used with a period of time; "since" is used with a starting point.
  - for 5 years / since 2020.
