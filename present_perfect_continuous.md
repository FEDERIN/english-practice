# Present Perfect Continuous

## When to use it

1. **Actions that started in the past and are still continuing**.
   - I have been studying English. (I'm still studying.)
   - She has been working here for 3 years.

2. **Actions that just finished, with a visible result**.
   - I have been running. (I'm tired now.)
   - It has been raining. (The ground is wet.)

## Form

- **Affirmative**: have/has + been + verb-ing.
  - I/you/we/they + have + been + V-ing.
  - He/she/it + has + been + V-ing.

- **Negative**: haven't/hasn't + been + verb-ing.
  - I haven't been sleeping well.

- **Question**: Have/Has + subject + been + verb-ing?
  - Have you been waiting long?

## Common time markers

for, since, lately, recently, all day, all week.

## Examples

- I have been studying English.
- We have been waiting for the bus.
- You have been practicing English every day.
- I have been learning Spanish for 3 months.
- I have been looking for my keys.
- You have been reading for 1 hour.
- We have been swimming in the pool.
- You have been working on that project all week.
- They have been training for 4 hours.

## Watch out

- Stative verbs (know, like, want, need, believe) are not usually used in the continuous form. Use the present perfect instead.
  - I have known him for 5 years. (not "I have been knowing")
