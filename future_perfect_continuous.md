# Future Perfect Continuous

## When to use it

1. **An action that will have been in progress for a period of time before a specific future moment**.
   - By next month, I will have been working here for 5 years.
   - We will have been working for five hours by then.

2. **To emphasize the duration of an action up to a future point**.
   - She will have been studying for 3 hours when the exam starts.

## Form

- **Affirmative**: will + have + been + verb-ing.
  - I/you/he/she/it/we/they + will + have + been + V-ing.

- **Negative**: won't + have + been + verb-ing.
  - I won't have been waiting long.

- **Question**: Will + subject + have + been + verb-ing?
  - Will you have been working all day?

## Common time markers

by, by the time, for, since, when, in (a year).

## Examples

- We will have been working for five hours.
- They will have been traveling for six hours.
- They will have been training for four hours.

## Watch out

- Do not confuse with the future perfect: the future perfect continuous emphasizes the duration of the action, not just its completion.
